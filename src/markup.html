01 · Meaning

Managementwith a humanside.

It grew in the 1920s–30s as a reaction to the classical approach, which treated workers almost like parts of a machine.

It gives importance to human behaviour at work, combining efficiency with the psychological and social needs of employees. That’s why it’s also called the human relations approach.

It focuses on
Human behaviour
Motivation
Leadership
Communication
Group relations
Participation

Contributor · Hawthorne studies

Elton Mayo

The Hawthorne studies 1924–1932

Western Electric’s Hawthorne Works, Chicago. Researchers dimmed the lights — and output still went up.

Social relationships at work affect output
Group behaviour shapes how hard people work
Attention from management raised productivity — the “Hawthorne effect”
Employee attitudes influence performance

Contributor · Leadership styles

Kurt Lewin

Groups shape how each person behaves, and so does the way they are led.

Autocratic

Leader decides alone

Quick decisions, but people feel controlled and morale can fall.

Worked best

Democratic

Group shares decisions

Employees take part, so satisfaction and commitment rise.

Laissez-faire

Group decides freely

Full freedom; works only with skilled, self-driven teams.

Contributor · Theory X and Theory Y

Douglas McGregor

Two opposite ways a manager can see a worker (1960).

X
The traditional view
  • Employees dislike work
  • They avoid responsibility
  • They need close supervision
Y
The human view
  • Work feels as natural as play or rest
  • People accept responsibility
  • They use self-direction and creativity

McGregor favoured Theory Y, the heart of the neo-classical approach.

06 · Limitations and conclusion

Not a perfect fix.

01May overemphasise social and psychological factors
02Human behaviour is complex and hard to predict
03Human relations alone can’t solve every problem
04People respond differently to the same motivation
05Economic, technological and environmental factors matter too
Conclusion

It added the human side to management: efficiency and well-being can go together.
